Summary
  Q1 (does sleep help?): no significant difference from no_sleep. fixed_letta vs no_sleep: median paired difference in final best normalized score +0.000 (95% CI -0.015 to +0.251), Wilcoxon p=0.75 (NOT significant), W/T/L 2/1/1 over 4 pairs. Q1 at an equal evaluator-call count (replays included): fixed_letta vs no_sleep: median paired difference in best normalized score at the pair's matched evaluator-call count +0.000 (95% CI -0.015 to +0.251), Wilcoxon p=0.75 (NOT significant), W/T/L 2/1/1 over 4 pairs. Q2 (does fatigue timing beat fixed schedules?): not testable (arms missing). Primary outcome: final best normalized score; significance at alpha=0.05, two-sided. With only 4 pairs no exact Wilcoxon test can reach p<0.05 (minimum p = 2/2^n), so every result here is not significant by construction; run more seeds.

What the results support:
  - Underpowered: 4 pair(s) per contrast; no '~' or '>' pattern of the guide can be assessed (need >= 6 pairs for p < 0.05 to be attainable).

Interpretation guide
  - fatigue > fixed_matched                      -> timing matters, beyond the amount of sleep
  - fatigue ~ fixed_matched, both > fixed_letta  -> the gain comes from sleeping less often, not from smart timing
  - fixed_matched ~ random_matched               -> fixed schedules carry no timing benefit
  - any sleep arm < no_sleep                     -> consolidation costs more budget than it returns at this run length
  - dream > fixed_letta                          -> REM dreaming + A/B validation beats NREM-only (the central claim)
  - dream ~ dream_unvalidated                    -> the hypotheses help even untested; validation is not load-bearing
  - dream < dream_unvalidated                    -> validation evals cost more budget than confirmed discoveries return
  ('>' / '<' = significant paired difference at alpha; '~' = not significant)
